Tesla and this month's hot topics

Why Tesla is priced on robots instead of cars, how the Anthropic IPO works, which story stocks are hot and which have cooled, and the rising interest rates that sit underneath all of it.

If you only read one part

The short version

  • Tesla's car business is flat; its stock is a bet on what comes next. Last quarter revenue was flat and the operating margin was 2.6%. The value rests on robotaxis, Optimus robots and a possible merger with SpaceX.
  • Anthropic goes public this week, raising up to about $64 billion at a value of up to about $1.4 trillion. It's a live example of how an IPO works.
  • Story stocks are cooling. Quantum, nuclear and robot companies have big stories and small revenue. Several are down 60% or more this year.
  • Interest rates are going up, not down. The Fed hiked in September and is expected to hike again on Dec 9. The 10-year yield is above 5%. That's the headwind for every expensive stock on this page.

Why a 2.6% margin company is worth so much. The price reflects what investors expect Tesla to earn years from now if robotaxis and robots work at scale. That's why autonomy news moves the stock more than car sales, and why it swings so hard on any delay.

Part B

The Anthropic IPO ANT

Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude, the AI that helped write these notes. This section sticks to reported facts and how IPOs work. Read other coverage before forming a view, and treat nothing here as a recommendation.

How an IPO works, using this one

  1. File the paperwork. The company files an S-1 with the SEC, disclosing its finances and risks. Anthropic's is public.

  2. Roadshow. Executives pitch big investors, and the banks set a price range: $70–78 a share here.

  3. Build the book. Investors place orders. Demand for this deal is reportedly several times the shares available.

  4. Price it. The night before trading, the company and banks set the final price. Expected Wednesday, Nov 18.

  5. First trade. The stock opens wherever buy and sell orders meet, often well above the IPO price for a popular deal. Expected Thursday, Nov 19.

  6. Lock-up ends. Insiders and early investors are barred from selling for a set period. When that ends, extra supply can weigh on the price.

Three ideas this IPO teaches. Run-rate is recent revenue multiplied out to a year, so it shows pace, not a full year of results. Dual-class control means public buyers can own most of the shares while founders keep most of the votes. And the chip makers buying shares alongside the IPO are also Anthropic's suppliers, an example of the "circular" money flows some investors worry about in AI.

Why rates matter so much for this page. A stock's price is roughly what investors think its future profits are worth today. When safe government bonds pay 5%, money far in the future is worth less in comparison. That hits companies whose profits are mostly in the future hardest: Tesla, new IPOs, and every story stock above. Higher rates also raise borrowing costs for capital-hungry builders.

  • The Fed is fighting inflation again. It raised rates in September for the first time since 2023 and held in October, with two officials voting for another hike. After October's hot inflation report, markets treat a Dec 9 hike as near-certain, and Governor Waller has floated a bigger half-point move. Kevin Warsh is Fed Chair.

Reference

Words in this sector

Deliveries
Cars handed to customers in a quarter. Tesla reports them a few days after each quarter ends, before full earnings.
Regulatory credits
Payments other carmakers make to Tesla to meet emissions rules. Pure profit, but shrinking.
Run-rate
Recent revenue multiplied out to a full year. Shows pace, not results.
Dual-class shares
Share classes with different voting power, letting founders keep control.
Lock-up
A period after an IPO when insiders can't sell.
Basis point (bp)
One hundredth of a percent. A 25bp hike raises rates by 0.25%.
Core CPI
Consumer inflation without food and energy, which jump around. The Fed watches it closely.
Bond yield
The interest a bond pays at today's price. When yields rise, bond prices fall and stocks usually face pressure.

1. Where does most of Tesla's revenue come from today?

2. Tesla's operating margin was 2.6%. Why is it still valued far above other carmakers?

The valuation is mostly about expected future businesses, which is why autonomy news moves the stock more than car sales.

3. Anthropic cites a run-rate of about $81B. What does run-rate mean?

Run-rate takes a recent period and annualizes it. It shows the current pace, which can change quickly.

4. Anthropic's founders hold 50.1% of the votes through one special share. What does that mean for public shareholders?

Dual-class structures separate ownership from control. Google and Meta use similar setups.

5. What is an IPO lock-up?

When a lock-up ends, insiders can sell, adding supply. Stocks often wobble around those dates.

6. Why does a hot inflation report tend to hurt high-growth tech stocks?

Higher yields shrink the present value of far-off profits. Companies whose profits lie mostly in the future feel it most.

7. Agility Robotics went public through a SPAC. What is a SPAC?

SPACs are faster than a traditional IPO, with less scrutiny, and many have performed poorly after the merger.
