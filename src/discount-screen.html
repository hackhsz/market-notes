If you only read one part

The short version

  • Analyst targets show +50% to +250% "upside" on many names. Targets lag sell-offs and usually get cut afterward. Treat them as stale.
How to read it

Three meanings of "on discount"

1 · PriceBelow its high

How far the price is from its 52-week high. Easy to see, but it says nothing about value: a stock can be 70% off and still expensive, or near its high and cheap.

2 · ValuationBelow its usual multiple

Price ÷ next year's expected earnings (forward P/E), compared with the stock's own typical level over about 5 years. Unprofitable companies use price ÷ sales instead.

3 · OpinionBelow analyst targets

Where analysts think the price will be in 12 months. Useful for direction, but targets trail big moves and are often cut right after a drop.

The trap to watch: a P/E falls when the price falls, or when the expected earnings were too high. If earnings estimates get cut next, a "cheap" 12× can become an ordinary 20× without the price moving at all.

Before acting on any of this

Six questions for any "cheap" stock

  1. Why is it down? One-off news (a CEO exit, a sector sell-off) can pass. A shrinking business, rising debt or lost customers usually doesn't.
  2. Are earnings estimates being cut? If yes, the low P/E is partly an illusion. Watch the next report and analyst revisions.
  3. Can it pay its bills without new money? Negative cash flow plus heavy borrowing means more dilution or costly debt, especially with yields above 5%.
  4. Is it still falling? A downtrend can run much further than looks reasonable. The Momentum Lab shows whether a stock has stabilized above its averages.
  5. What's the next test, and when? An earnings date, a launch, a court ruling. Buying just before one means accepting a big move either way.
  6. How much, and where would you be wrong? Size it with Buying basics and write the exit before entering, in the exit-plan builder.
