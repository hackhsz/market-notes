AI chips, one layer deeper

How an AI chip actually works, the eight-step journey from design to data center, who earns what along the way, the big fight between Nvidia and custom chips, and exactly what to look for in Nvidia's earnings on Tuesday.

If you only read one part

The short version

  • AI is a mountain of simple multiplication. Chips that do thousands of multiplications at once, and get data in and out fast enough, win.
  • Nvidia sells whole systems, not just chips, and still earns most of the money in AI hardware. Custom chips built for Google, Amazon and others are growing faster, from a smaller base.
  • Everything runs through a few chokepoints: TSMC's factories and packaging, three memory makers, and one Dutch machine maker. Those companies win no matter which chip designer wins.
  • This week was unusual. Nvidia reported on Nov 17 under an interim CEO, after its founder resigned on Nov 12. Revenue and the forecast beat expectations, and the stock rose about 2% after hours. See the results.
The technology, without the jargon

How an AI chip works

Why GPUs? A neural network is mostly multiplying big grids of numbers. A regular processor (CPU) is like a few very smart workers who handle anything, one task at a time. A GPU is like thousands of simple workers doing the same kind of math at once. For AI, the crowd wins.

Training vs. inference. Training is teaching a model, once, on huge clusters for weeks or months. Inference is using the trained model every time someone asks it something. Inference keeps growing with every new user, which is why it is becoming the larger market, and why cheaper custom chips aimed at inference are gaining ground.

The memory wall. AI chips can do math faster than ordinary memory can feed them numbers, so they sit idle waiting. The fix is HBM: memory stacked in towers and placed millimeters from the chip. That's why memory makers are now AI companies too.

Many chips acting as one. No single chip can hold a modern model, so chips are wired together. Inside a rack, Nvidia's NVLink lets 72 GPUs behave like one giant GPU. Between racks, network switches tie thousands of them into a cluster.

Design to data center

The journey of one AI chip

Each step is a different set of companies. The red tags mark chokepoints, where only one to three companies in the world can do the job.

  1. Design NVDA · AMD · AVGO · MRVL

    Engineers draw the chip over two to three years, using design software from Synopsys (SNPS) and Cadence (CDNS). Some designs build on Arm's blueprints.

  2. Machines Chokepoint ASML

    ASML is the only company that makes EUV lithography machines, which print the finest patterns. Each one costs well over $100 million.

  3. Manufacturing Chokepoint TSM

    TSMC etches the chips onto silicon wafers, mostly in Taiwan with growing capacity in Arizona. Its newest 2nm process is ramping now.

  4. Memory Chokepoint SK Hynix · MU · Samsung

    Three companies make the HBM memory stacks. SK Hynix leads and says it is sold out through 2029.

  5. Packaging Chokepoint TSM

    TSMC's CoWoS process joins the chip and its memory on one base. For years this has been the tightest bottleneck in the whole chain.

  6. Systems Foxconn · Quanta · DELL · SMCI

    Contract manufacturers assemble chips into trays and full racks.

  7. Networking NVDA · AVGO · ANET

    Switches and cables tie racks together. Nvidia sells its own networking; Broadcom and Arista sell Ethernet gear.

  8. Customers

    Big Tech clouds, neoclouds like CoreWeave and IREN, AI labs, and governments building national AI systems.

The question that decides Nvidia's future

Nvidia GPUs vs. custom chips

Nvidia's biggest customers are also building their own chips: Google's TPU, Amazon's Trainium, Microsoft's Maia and Meta's MTIA. OpenAI unveiled its first in-house chip, built with Broadcom, this year. Most of these are designed with help from Broadcom or Marvell and manufactured by TSMC.

Why customers build their own

  • Cost: Nvidia's 75% margin is their expense
  • Tuned for their own workloads, especially inference
  • Less dependence on one supplier
  • They have the scale to justify a chip team

Why Nvidia still leads

  • CUDA: most AI software is written for it
  • Sells the full system: chips, networking, software
  • A new generation roughly every year
  • Flexible: runs any model, not just one company's

  • Share estimates vary by method. Research firms put Nvidia at roughly 75–85% of AI chip revenue in 2026, down from about 87% in 2024. Google's TPU is estimated at 6–8% and Amazon's Trainium at 2–3%.

    Safeguard · Hashrate Index

  • Custom chips are growing faster. TrendForce expects custom chip shipments to grow about 45% this year versus about 16% for GPUs. Amazon has reportedly cited more than $225B in Trainium commitments, including deals with Anthropic and OpenAI.

    Tech Insider · Safeguard

  • It isn't either/or yet. OpenAI kept its Nvidia and AMD deals while building its own chip, so custom chips have mostly added to GPU fleets so far.

    Tech Insider

Whoever wins the design fight, TSMC manufactures the chips, the memory makers supply the HBM, and Broadcom designs many of the custom chips. Investors call companies like these "picks and shovels."

Politics

China and export controls

The US limits which AI chips can be sold to China. Since late 2025 it has allowed Nvidia's older H200 to approved Chinese buyers, with the US government taking a 25% cut. But Beijing has largely blocked those imports.

  • Chips stuck in limbo. About 600,000 H200 chips are reportedly sitting in Hong Kong waiting for approval to enter mainland China. Nvidia took a $0.4B charge for excess H200 inventory in the first half of its fiscal year.

    Unite.AI (citing Reuters) · Built In

  • New US rules today. The Commerce Department issued a rule aimed at Singapore and Malaysia, part of a push to stop chips being rerouted to China.

    Fortune

  • What it means for the numbers. Nvidia's forecast assumes zero data center chip sales to China. Any China revenue would be a bonus, and any new restriction mostly hurts future hopes, not current sales.

How to read Nvidia's earnings

First, the calendar. Nvidia's fiscal year ends in late January, so "fiscal Q3 2027" means roughly August through October 2026. Many companies number their quarters this way, so always check the actual months.

How the evening goes. Nvidia posts its results shortly after the market closes, then holds a call where executives answer analyst questions. The stock trades and moves in after-hours trading right away, and the next regular session gives the real verdict.

Both sides, as investors argue them

The bull case and the bear case

Bull case

  • Demand still outruns supply: TSMC keeps raising forecasts and SK Hynix is sold out through 2029
  • Inference grows with every new AI user
  • Alphabet says its 2027 spending will rise significantly
  • Each new Nvidia generation earns more per data center
  • AMD's ramp shows demand is big enough for more than one winner

Bear case

  • Several Big Tech companies now spend more on capex than their operations bring in
  • Custom chips chip away at Nvidia's share and margins
  • Leadership uncertainty at the industry's most important company
  • China is closed for now, and the rules keep shifting
  • Chip booms have historically ended in oversupply
  • Interest rates above 5% make expensive stocks harder to justify
Reference

Words in this sector

CUDA
Nvidia's programming platform. Years of AI software are written for it, which makes switching costly.
NVLink
Nvidia's high-speed links that let many GPUs in a rack work as one.
CoWoS
TSMC's advanced packaging that places a chip and its memory side by side on one base.
EUV
Extreme ultraviolet lithography: the light-based printing that makes the smallest chip features. Only ASML builds the machines.
ASIC / XPU
A custom chip built for one customer's workload. Google's TPU is one.
Rack-scale
Selling a whole rack of chips, networking and cooling as one product, as Nvidia and AMD now do.
Sovereign AI
Governments buying AI computing for their own countries. A newer source of chip demand.
Whisper number
The unofficial figure traders actually expect, often above the published analyst consensus.
Check yourself

Seven-question quiz

Score: 0 of 7 answered correctly

1. Nvidia designs its AI chips. Who manufactures them?

Nvidia is fabless. TSMC manufactures its chips. ASML builds the machines TSMC uses, and Micron makes memory.

2. Why is HBM memory placed right next to the GPU?

AI chips do math faster than ordinary memory can supply numbers. Putting memory millimeters away shortens the trip. HBM is actually far more expensive than regular memory.

3. What is inference?

Training happens once per model. Inference happens every time someone uses it, so it grows with users. Many custom chips target inference.

4. Nvidia beats its revenue forecast, and the stock falls the next day. What's the most likely reason?

Prices already include what investors expect. A company can beat its own forecast and still fall short of the market's hopes, which has happened to Nvidia several times.

5. Which company helps Google design its TPU chips?

Broadcom co-designs custom chips for several giants, Google's TPU among them. That's why it rises on custom-chip news.

6. Nvidia's "fiscal Q3 2027" covers roughly which months?

Nvidia's fiscal year ends in late January, so fiscal 2027 mostly overlaps calendar 2026.

7. Which of these has only one supplier in the world?

Only ASML makes EUV machines. Chip design has several rivals, many firms assemble servers, and HBM has three makers.
