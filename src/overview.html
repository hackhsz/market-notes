The one idea that connects sectors 1, 2 and 4

Follow the money

Big Tech's spending on AI data centers is everyone else's revenue. When Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta raise their spending plans, the companies below them tend to rise together. When they cut, those companies fall together. That is why one Nvidia earnings report can move dozens of stocks.

Seven terms that come up everywhere

Market cap
Share price × number of shares. What the market says the whole company is worth.
Revenue growth
How much more a company sold than a year earlier. Faster growth usually earns a higher valuation.
Gross margin
The share of each sales dollar left after the direct cost of making the product. High margins signal pricing power.
P/E ratio
Share price ÷ yearly profit per share: roughly how many years of today's profit you are paying for. "Forward P/E" uses next year's expected profit.
Capex
Capital expenditure: money spent on long-lasting things like buildings, chips and power plants.
Free cash flow
Cash left after running the business and paying capex. Big Tech's is shrinking now because AI capex is so large.
Guidance
A company's own forecast for the next quarter or year. Stocks often react more to guidance than to the results themselves.
Earnings report
The results every public company publishes four times a year, followed by a call where management takes questions. The big moves happen the next day.
Sector 1

AI chips

Go deeper: AI Chips Deep Dive →

Building a modern chip takes three different kinds of company. Designers like Nvidia draw the chip but own no factory. A foundry, almost always TSMC for the most advanced chips, manufactures it. Equipment makers like ASML build the machines the foundry uses.

AI runs on accelerators: Nvidia's GPUs, AMD's competing GPUs, or custom chips that Broadcom co-designs for companies like Google. Each one is packaged next to stacks of fast memory (more on that in sector 2). Nvidia's edge is partly hardware and partly CUDA, the software most AI developers already write for.

Words you'll hear

Fabless
A chip company that designs but doesn't manufacture. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom are fabless.
Node (3nm, 2nm)
A generation of manufacturing technology. Smaller numbers mean newer, denser, more efficient chips.
GPU vs. ASIC
A GPU is a general-purpose accelerator. An ASIC is a custom chip built for one customer's workload.
Data center revenue
Nvidia's most watched line: sales of AI chips and systems to cloud and AI companies.
Export controls
US rules limiting which advanced chips can be sold to China. Changes can swing revenue.
Cyclical
Chip demand has always moved in booms and busts. Investors argue over whether AI changes that.

What moves these stocks

  • Big Tech raising or cutting its spending plans
  • Data center revenue and gross margin at earnings
  • Supply limits at TSMC and in memory
  • China export rules
  • New chip generations, and now Nvidia's leadership

Risks

  • A handful of huge customers buy most AI chips
  • Boom-and-bust history of chip demand
  • Most advanced manufacturing is concentrated in Taiwan
  • Prices already assume years of growth
Sector 2

Memory, data centers and power

Go deeper: Memory & Power Deep Dive →

An AI data center needs more than chips. It needs memory, a building, cooling, and an enormous amount of electricity. Each of those has become its own AI investment theme.

Memory. HBM (high-bandwidth memory) is ordinary DRAM memory stacked into towers and placed right beside each GPU so it can be fed data fast enough. Only three companies make it at scale: SK Hynix, the leader and Nvidia's main supplier, plus Samsung and Micron.

Data centers. Someone has to own the building and the power hookup. Some former bitcoin miners, IREN among them, already had both, and now rent GPU capacity to tech giants. These are often called neoclouds.

Power. A large AI campus can draw as much electricity as a small city. Getting a grid connection is now often the slowest step, so power plant owners and equipment makers trade like AI stocks too.

Words you'll hear

HBM / HBM4
High-bandwidth memory and its newest generation. The scarcest part of an AI chip package.
Wafer
A silicon disc that chips are cut from. Memory capacity is quoted in wafers per month.
MW / GW
Megawatts and gigawatts. AI data centers are now sized by how much power they draw, not by floor space.
Neocloud
A newer company that rents GPU capacity, separate from Amazon, Microsoft and Google.
PPA
Power purchase agreement: a long-term contract to buy electricity at a set price.
Backlog
Orders signed but not yet delivered. A growing backlog means future revenue is lined up.

  • SK Hynix is still expanding. It says its HBM capacity is essentially sold out through 2029. Korean press reports a plan to add at least 20,000 HBM4 wafers a month; the company hasn't confirmed it.

    FourWeekMBA (citing ETNews) · TrendForce

  • Memory stocks have dropped anyway. SK Hynix's US shares are down about 16% over the past month and Micron about 21%. Analysts worry that new capacity from all three makers lands in 2028–29 just as HBM growth slows.

    Yahoo Finance · 24/7 Wall St.

  • Antitrust case moves ahead. On Nov 14 a US judge refused to dismiss a class action accusing Samsung, Micron and SK Hynix of fixing DRAM prices.

    Wccftech

  • IREN's Microsoft deal is being delivered. The 5-year, $9.7B contract (signed Nov 2025, 20% prepaid) gives Microsoft Nvidia GPU capacity in Texas. Microsoft has accepted the first of four 50MW phases. IREN targets 480MW of AI cloud capacity in 2026 and 1.2GW in 2027.

    IREN release · StockTitan

  • Power stocks fell after the midterms. After Democrats swept the midterm elections, Vistra fell 15.4%, GE Vernova 11.5% and Constellation 9.2% in a day, while renewable energy stocks rallied.

    Yahoo Finance

What moves these stocks

  • Big Tech spending plans, again
  • New long-term contracts, like IREN's with Microsoft
  • Memory prices and supply
  • Power prices, energy policy and politics
  • Interest rates, since these companies borrow heavily to build

Risks

  • Memory has a long history of booms followed by gluts
  • Heavy reliance on one or two customers
  • Expensive debt with Treasury yields above 5%
  • If AI spending slows, this layer feels it first
Sector 3

Games

Game companies make money four ways. They sell games up front (a $70 console release). They sell extras inside free games, like skins or Robux. They sell subscriptions such as Game Pass. And platforms take a cut: consoles and app stores typically keep about 30% of each sale.

It's a hits business. One big release can make or break a publisher's year, so release dates, delays and launch sales move the stocks. This sector mostly runs on its own calendar, separate from the AI money chain above.

Words you'll hear

Net bookings
Sales made in a period, including digital purchases not yet counted as revenue. The industry's headline number.
Free-to-play
Free to download, paid through in-game purchases. Fortnite and most Roblox games work this way.
DAU
Daily active users. Roblox reports it every quarter.
Bookings per DAU
How much the average daily player spends. Rising engagement with falling spending is a warning sign.
Live service
A game updated for years with new content and purchases, instead of sold once.
Console cycle
Sales surge after a new console launches, then fade until the next one.

What moves these stocks

  • Release dates, and especially delays
  • Launch sales and player counts
  • Bookings guidance
  • Engagement and spending per player
  • Consumer spending overall

Risks

  • One flop or delay can sink a year
  • Budgets for big games keep climbing
  • Players cut back on extras when money is tight
  • Regulation of child safety and paid loot boxes
Sector 4

Big Tech

Each of these companies has a profit engine that pays for its AI ambitions. Microsoft sells Office and its Azure cloud. Alphabet sells search and YouTube ads plus Google Cloud. Amazon has its store, ads and AWS. Meta sells ads across Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. Apple sells iPhones and services.

Amazon, Microsoft and Google also run the three big clouds, renting computing power to everyone else. The question hanging over all of them right now: will AI earn back the record amounts they are spending on data centers?

Together with Nvidia and Tesla, these make up the "Magnificent Seven," the stocks that drive much of the S&P 500.

Words you'll hear

Hyperscaler
A company running cloud data centers at giant scale: Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and often Meta.
Cloud growth
The growth rates of Azure, AWS and Google Cloud. Probably the most watched numbers each quarter.
Operating cash flow
Cash the business generates before capex. Compare it with capex to see whether spending is self-funded.
Finance leases
Data centers rented long-term instead of bought. Some trackers count them as capex and some don't, which is why totals differ.

What's happening now · from late-October earnings

  • Record spending. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft spent roughly $202–209B on capex combined in one quarter, depending on whether leases are counted.

    Luminix · Technology.org

  • Microsoft stood out. Azure grew 48%, it trimmed its capex forecast to $170B from $175B, and its operating cash flow covered its spending.

    Averin · Technology.org

  • Alphabet spent more than it took in. Capex was $51.4B against $42.3B of operating cash flow. It kept its 2026 range of $195–205B and said 2027 will rise significantly. The stock fell about 13% that day, its worst on record.

    Averin

  • Amazon and Meta both fell on earnings. AWS grew 39%, its fastest in nearly five years, but quarterly capex hit $77.3B and the holiday forecast was weak. Meta's revenue grew 26%, but its free cash flow turned negative and shares dropped about 7%.

    AppleScoop · Technology.org

These figures come from trackers that don't always agree with each other. The companies' 10-Q filings are the final word.

What moves these stocks

  • Cloud growth rates
  • Capex guidance, both up and down
  • Ad revenue (Google, Meta, Amazon)
  • Free cash flow
  • Antitrust rulings

Risks

  • Spending running ahead of AI revenue
  • More borrowing to fund data centers
  • Regulators breaking up or restricting businesses
  • AI chatbots eating into search ads
Sector 5

Tesla and this month's hot topics

Tesla TSLA

Tesla sells electric cars and big batteries for homes and power grids. But most of its stock value is a bet on two future businesses: robotaxis and Optimus humanoid robots. That's why it's valued far above other car makers, and why autonomy news moves the stock more than car sales do.

  • Robotaxi is in 8 cities, 5 in Texas and 3 in Florida, after San Antonio launched Nov 9. Most cars are Model Ys; the purpose-built Cybercab runs only in Austin. Texas lists 1,302 registered Tesla autonomous vehicles against Waymo's 1,297, though registered doesn't mean in service.

    OhSoft weekly · Tesla Q3 10-Q

  • Optimus will probably be rented, not sold, according to Musk. He estimates each robot costs Tesla about $30,000 to build, falling toward $20,000. Deliveries to outside companies are targeted around the end of 2027.

    Not a Tesla App

  • Analysts are split on the valuation, citing Optimus delays, weak cash generation, heavy spending needs and Waymo's head start.

    TokenPost

Watch: quarterly deliveries, car gross margin, FSD adoption, new robotaxi cities and safety data, and the energy storage business.
